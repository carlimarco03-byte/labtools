What is qPCR?

Quantitative PCR (qPCR), also known as real-time PCR, is a molecular biology technique used to monitor DNA amplification during the reaction.


Unlike conventional PCR, where amplification is typically evaluated at the end of the reaction, qPCR monitors fluorescence during successive amplification cycles.

Main qPCR components

DNA Template

The DNA containing the sequence of interest. For RT-qPCR experiments, the starting material is RNA that has first been converted into complementary DNA.

Forward & Reverse Primers

Primers define the target region and provide the starting points for DNA synthesis.

DNA Polymerase

A thermostable DNA polymerase synthesizes new DNA during the amplification process.

dNTPs

Deoxynucleotide triphosphates provide the building blocks required for DNA synthesis.

Reaction Buffer

The buffer provides the appropriate chemical environment for the polymerase and amplification reaction.

Fluorescence Detection

A fluorescent signal allows the accumulation of amplified DNA to be monitored during the reaction.

SYBR Green vs probe-based qPCR

🟢 SYBR Green

SYBR Green is a fluorescent dye that binds to double-stranded DNA.


As the amount of double-stranded DNA increases, the fluorescence signal increases.


SYBR Green assays are relatively simple and cost-effective, but fluorescence is not inherently specific to the intended amplicon.

🔵 Probe-based qPCR

Probe-based assays use a sequence-specific fluorescent probe in addition to the forward and reverse primers.


The probe provides an additional layer of sequence specificity during detection.


These assays can be more specific, but generally require additional assay design and reagents.

Primer concentrations

Primer concentrations are usually specified as final concentrations in the reaction. The required volume can be calculated from the primer stock concentration using:

C₁ × V₁ = C₂ × V₂

For example, using a 20 µM primer stock in a 20 µL reaction with a desired final concentration of 0.5 µM:

V₁ = (0.5 µM × 20 µL) / 20 µM
Primer volume

0.5 µL per reaction

Probe concentration

In probe-based qPCR, the fluorescent probe is an additional component of the reaction and its final concentration must be considered separately from the primer concentrations.


The appropriate concentration depends on the assay, probe chemistry and manufacturer's recommendations.

Master mix

Commercial qPCR master mixes commonly contain several reaction components, such as DNA polymerase, dNTPs, buffer and fluorescent detection chemistry.


When using a 2X master mix, a common setup is to use it at a final concentration of 1X.

C₁ × V₁ = C₂ × V₂

For example, in a 20 µL reaction using a 2X master mix at a final concentration of 1X:

2X Master Mix

10 µL per 20 µL reaction

Important considerations

Amplicon specificity

Primer design and assay specificity are important for obtaining reliable amplification and fluorescence signals.

Controls

Appropriate controls, such as no-template controls, help identify contamination or non-specific signals.

Reaction efficiency

qPCR performance should be evaluated using appropriate assay validation procedures rather than relying only on the calculated reaction volumes.

Instrument settings

Fluorescence acquisition and analysis depend on the qPCR instrument and chemistry being used.
