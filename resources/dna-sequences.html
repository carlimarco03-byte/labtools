DNA Sequences

A practical introduction to DNA sequences, complementary strands, reverse complements and basic sequence analysis.

What is a DNA sequence?

DNA sequences are ordered combinations of four nucleotide bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C) and guanine (G).


Adenine (A)

Pairs with thymine (T).

Thymine (T)

Pairs with adenine (A).

Cytosine (C)

Pairs with guanine (G).

Guanine (G)

Pairs with cytosine (C).

DNA strand orientation

DNA strands have a direction determined by the 5' and 3' ends of the nucleotide backbone. The two strands of DNA are antiparallel.


5' — A T G C — 3'

3' — T A C G — 5'

The sequence of one strand therefore determines the complementary sequence of the other strand.

Complementary sequences

To obtain the complementary sequence, replace each nucleotide with its corresponding base:

A → T
T → A
C → G
G → C


For example:

5' — A T G C C A — 3'

3' — T A C G G T — 5'

Reverse complement

The reverse complement is obtained by first generating the complementary sequence and then reversing its orientation so that the result is also written from 5' to 3'.


Original: 5' — A T G C C A — 3'

Complement: 3' — T A C G G T — 5'

Reverse complement: 5' — T G G C A T — 3'

Reverse-complement sequences are commonly used when working with DNA primers, sequence databases, PCR assays and molecular cloning workflows.

GC content

GC content represents the percentage of nucleotides in a DNA sequence that are either guanine (G) or cytosine (C).

GC% = (G + C) / total nucleotides × 100

For example, consider:

5' — A T G C C A — 3'

There are 3 G/C nucleotides out of 6 total nucleotides.

GC% = 3 / 6 × 100 = 50%

Why sequence orientation matters

Many molecular biology applications require DNA sequences to be written in a specific orientation, normally from 5' to 3'.


This is particularly important when designing PCR primers, comparing sequences or searching sequence databases.


A sequence and its reverse complement contain the same genetic information, but they represent opposite strands and orientations.

Common sequence checks

Sequence length

Count the number of nucleotides in the sequence.

GC content

Determine the percentage of G and C nucleotides.

Reverse complement

Generate the sequence corresponding to the opposite DNA strand in 5' to 3' orientation.
