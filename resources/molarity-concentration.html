Molarity & Concentration

Understand molarity, mass concentration, molecular weight and solution volume in laboratory calculations.

What is molarity?

Molarity describes the amount of a substance dissolved in a solution relative to the total solution volume.


M = n / V

where M is the molarity in mol/L, n is the amount of substance in moles, and V is the solution volume in liters.

Calculating moles

When the mass of a compound and its molecular weight are known, the amount of substance can be calculated using:

n = m / MW

where m is the mass of the compound and MW is its molecular weight in g/mol.

Preparing a solution from a solid

To prepare a solution at a desired molarity, the required mass of a compound can be calculated from the target concentration, molecular weight and final volume.

m = M × MW × V

The volume must be expressed in liters when using molarity in mol/L.


Example

Suppose you want to prepare 100 mL of a 0.5 M solution of a compound with a molecular weight of 100 g/mol.


First convert the volume:

100 mL = 0.1 L

Then:

m = 0.5 × 100 × 0.1 = 5 g

Therefore, 5 g of the compound are required to prepare 100 mL of a 0.5 M solution.

Molarity and mass concentration

Concentration can also be expressed as mass per unit volume, for example g/L or mg/mL. The relationship between molarity and mass concentration depends on the molecular weight of the compound.

mass concentration = M × MW

For example, a 1 M solution of a compound with a molecular weight of 100 g/mol corresponds to 100 g/L.

Common concentration units

M

Moles per liter (mol/L).

mM

Millimoles per liter.

µM

Micromoles per liter.

g/L

Grams of solute per liter of solution.

mg/mL

Milligrams of solute per milliliter of solution.

µg/mL

Micrograms of solute per milliliter of solution.

Important considerations

When preparing laboratory solutions, always check the molecular weight, hydrate state, purity and required final volume of the compound.


For concentrated stock solutions and reagents, also consider the manufacturer's specifications and the actual concentration of the supplied product.


Calculations should be independently checked before preparing solutions used in experiments.
