Concentration Units

Understand molarity, mass concentration and the most common concentration units used in laboratory calculations.

What is concentration?

Concentration describes the amount of a substance present in a defined amount of solution. Different scientific applications use different ways of expressing concentration.


In laboratory work, two of the most common approaches are molar concentration and mass concentration.

Molar concentration

Molarity expresses the amount of substance in moles per liter of solution.

M = n / V

where:

  • M = molarity (mol/L)
  • n = amount of substance (mol)
  • V = solution volume (L)

Example

If 0.5 mol of a compound is dissolved to make 1 L of solution:

M = 0.5 mol / 1 L = 0.5 M

Common molarity units

M

Molar concentration expressed in moles per liter.

1 M = 1 mol/L

mM

Millimolar.

1 mM = 10⁻³ M

µM

Micromolar.

1 µM = 10⁻⁶ M

Therefore:

1 M = 1000 mM = 1,000,000 µM

Mass concentration

Mass concentration expresses the mass of solute present in a defined volume of solution.

Mass concentration = mass / volume

Common units include:

g/L

Grams per liter.

mg/mL

Milligrams per milliliter.

µg/mL

Micrograms per milliliter.

Converting between mass and molar concentration

To convert between molarity and mass concentration, the molecular weight of the compound is required.


g/L = M × MW

where MW is the molecular weight in g/mol.

Example: NaCl

The molecular weight of NaCl is approximately 58.44 g/mol. Suppose the solution concentration is 0.5 M.

g/L = 0.5 mol/L × 58.44 g/mol

g/L = 29.22 g/L

Therefore, a 0.5 M NaCl solution corresponds to 29.22 g/L.

Useful mass concentration relationships

Some laboratory units are numerically equivalent because of the relationship between their volume and mass units.

1 mg/mL = 1 g/L

1 µg/mL = 1 mg/L

These relationships are useful when working with different protocols, datasheets and reagent labels.

Choosing the appropriate unit

Molar units

M, mM and µM are useful when the chemical amount of a compound is important, particularly in stoichiometric calculations and biochemical assays.

Mass units

g/L, mg/mL and µg/mL are useful when concentrations are specified directly as mass per volume.

Check the protocol

Always use the units specified by the experimental protocol and verify conversions before preparing solutions.

Common conversion mistakes

1. Forgetting the molecular weight

Molar concentration cannot be converted into mass concentration without knowing the molecular weight of the compound.


2. Mixing mL and L

Remember that:

1 L = 1000 mL

Incorrect volume units can introduce errors of three orders of magnitude.


3. Confusing µM and mM

The prefixes represent different powers of ten:

1 mM = 1000 µM
